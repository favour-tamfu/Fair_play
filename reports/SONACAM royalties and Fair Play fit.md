# Fair Play supplies the key SONACAM lacks

SONACAM already handles membership, work documentation, collection and payment. What it lacks is any usage data to split the bar money it now collects. Since February 2026 that money arrives through a volume levy of 12 FCFA per beer crate, withheld at source by drinks distributors. The levy carries no playlist, so the board still splits it with flat sums and tiers it sets itself. Fair Play should therefore join SONACAM as a paid supplier of usage data and a statistical split key, not as a second rights society or a payment system. SONACAM keeps every function the law gives it: its mandate, its repertoire, its distribution rules, its decisions on rights-holder shares, and the payouts. Fair Play adds a sampled panel of meters in bars, standardised and extrapolated shares with error ranges, an explicit "music, unidentified" share, and an audit trail it can show. It hands these over as flat files keyed on ISRC, ISWC and SONACAM's own work codes, ready for WIPO Connect. This matters because the bar channel is now one of SONACAM's largest sources of income. The levy reportedly brought in 100M FCFA by July 2026, against a historical baseline of a few hundred million a year. Meanwhile, artist anger over the 27,000 FCFA flat advance and the 2,000 FCFA payment is the sector's main political problem. Four risks could still sink the project if the team ignores them. First, the levy is shared among five collective management organisations (CMOs), so the pot is not all SONACAM's. Second, bar unions struck in August 2026 against the levy. Third, Cameroon's new data-protection law has been in force since June 2026. Fourth, a meter panel cannot match local repertoire that has never been digitised. Every money figure in this report is press-reported unless marked otherwise. Only the statute's articles were checked against primary text.

## SONACAM already runs everything except usage measurement

SONACAM is a non-profit civil society, approved for category B (music) by ministerial decision N°0012/MINAC of **21 December 2017** after a constitutive general assembly on 9 September 2017. It is the state-imposed successor to the feuding CMC and SOCAM ([sonacam.org](https://www.sonacam.org); [IFACCA](https://www.ifacca.org/news/2017/12/22/cameroons-authors-rights-sonacam-gets-accreditatio/)). Its legal position is a monopoly. Law 2000/011 allows only one CMO per category (Art. 75). Joining automatically gives the CMO a mandate to license, collect, distribute and sue (Art. 77) ([Law 2000/011, OAPI](https://oapi.int/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Loi_19decembre_2000Cameroun.pdf)). Since a 2015 amendment, MINAC grants or withdraws approval only "après visa du Premier ministre" ([Camer.be](https://www.camer.be/amp/42355/11:1/cameroun-le-pm-donne-le-tempo-sur-le-droit-dauteur-de-lart-musical-cameroon.html)). Dr Francis Ateh Bazore has chaired the board since December 2020. He was re-elected on 30 April 2026 with **733 votes to 45** ([allAfrica](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202605010511.html)). The Commission de contrôle des organismes de gestion collective (CCOGC), chaired by Pr François Edimo in 2026, oversees the scheme ([Cameroon Tribune](https://www.cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/76231/fr.html/organismes-de-gestion-collective-du-droit-dauteur-on-evalue-les-performances)). Governance has been turbulent. In 2022 a general assembly was banned and the Mfoundi court appointed provisional administrators, an order that MINAC and the board appealed ([Cameroon Tribune](https://www.cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/49423/en.html/droits-dauteur-la-nouvelle-affaire-sonacam)).

SONACAM manages **authors' rights only**: those of lyricists, composers and publishers. Performers and producers fall under the neighbouring-rights society SCDV. This matters for Fair Play's language. The product pays the *rights holders of a work*, not "the artist" who is heard. Membership is on paper. Applicants submit an ID, photos, a CD of 5–6 original tracks and a form. An identification commission listens to the CD, and the reported fee is 10,000 FCFA. That last figure comes from a secondary blog, and accounts conflict on whether the fee is paid upfront or withheld at source ([Muzisecur](https://www.muzisecur.fr/blog/sonacam-adhesion-procedure-cameroun-auteur-compositeur/); [Saimondy](https://www.saimondy.com/2017/10/08/sonacam-artistes-camerounais/)). About **1,000 artists** were still waiting for identification in June 2023 ([Cameroon Tribune](https://www.cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/58312/fr.html/droits-dauteur-dans-lart-musical-notes-despoir)). In May 2026 the board chair claimed **7,734 members** and **about 105,000 works** documented "in a physical and digital database" in WIPO Connect ([Cameroon Tribune](https://www.cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/76344/en.html/details_2)). Elsewhere he spoke of "about 3,000 members" ([allAfrica](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202609230156.html)), so neither figure is reliable. SONACAM's site says the WIPO Connect agreement was signed on **7 July 2021** ([sonacam.org](https://www.sonacam.org)). No WIPO page confirms that the system is deployed. No source shows members receiving IPI numbers or works receiving ISWCs, and the site's "Déclarer une œuvre" page returned a 404 error on 24 November 2026 ([attempted URL](https://www.sonacam.org/declarer-une-oeuvre)). Members are identified by a "numéro de mandat". They collect cash at **Express Union Finance** agencies on presenting ID ([Cameroun24](https://cameroun24.net/article/70951-sonacam-debut-des-paiements-de-la-r-partition-du-30-septembre-chaque-artiste-recevra-27-000-fcfa.html)).

### Collection moved to source, but the money arrives without a playlist

Bar money used to be collected through flat fees by venue type and size, enforced by field agents, bailiffs and seizures of equipment. A 2009 MINAC decision set those fees at 29,500–85,000 FCFA ([237online](https://www.237online.com/droit-dauteur-la-collecte-des-fonds-facilite-auprs-des-tenanciers-des-bars/)). Joint decision **n°00034/MINAC/MINCOMMERCE of 25 April 2023** replaced that system with a deduction at source of **1 FCFA per bottle, or 12 FCFA per crate**. Large distributors withhold it and pay it into a special deposit account ([Relais 237](https://relais237.com/revolution-dans-le-droit-dauteur-au-cameroun-la-redevance-a-la-source-met-fin-aux-contentieux)). The scheme was put in place in two steps. A protocol with Boissons du Cameroun followed on **6 October 2025**. On **13 February 2026** SONACAM, SCDV, SCAAP, SOCADAP and the barmen's union SYNDEBARCAM signed a convention ([Glob'Média](https://globmedia.online/2026/08/11/bras-de-fer-entre-les-barmans-et-la-sonacam-12-francs-cfa-de-la-discorde/); [Le Monde Professionnel](https://mondeprofessionnel.press/prestations-dans-les-debits-de-boisson-les-droits-dauteurs-des-artistes-dorenavant-preleves-a-la-source/)). The proceeds are shared among **five CMOs**, and one source says **4% goes to a MINAC culture fund**. No source gives the split key between the CMOs. In July 2026 the board chair announced **100M FCFA** collected through the levy ([Cameroon Tribune](https://www.cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/79271/en.html/copyrights-windfall-sonacam-secures-landmark-100-million-fcfa)). Historically, collections were described as "barely 500M FCFA/year" across copyright ([Ecomatin](https://ecomatin.net/droit-dauteur-entre-40-et-100-milliards-de-fcfa-a-recouvrer)). The bar channel is therefore now material, and Cameroon Tribune calls CRTV the only broadcaster that "really" pays ([Cameroon Tribune](https://www.cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/76554/fr.html/collecte-du-droit-dauteur-une-assiette-dusagers-elargir)). The private-copying levy is in the law but has never been collected ([Cameroon Tribune](https://www.cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/82149/fr.html/details_2)).

### Distribution is discretionary, and SONACAM says so itself

No règlement de répartition has been published. Recent rounds have used flat sums or board-set tiers: 25,000 FCFA per artist in December 2019, a 25,000–300,000 FCFA "grille" in June 2023, and a flat **27,000 FCFA "avance"** from a **40M FCFA** envelope in October 2025 ([StopBlaBlaCam](https://www.stopblablacam.com/culture-and-society/1501-3223-no-the-xaf25-000-distributed-to-musicians-on-dec-31-is-not-copyright-royalties); [Cameroun24](https://cameroun24.net/article/70951-sonacam-debut-des-paiements-de-la-r-partition-du-30-septembre-chaque-artiste-recevra-27-000-fcfa.html)). Eligibility reportedly depends on membership category, notoriety, appearing in broadcast registers and holding a leadership role ([237online](https://www.237online.com/droit-dauteurs-la-sonacam-a-la-croisee-des-chemins/)). In 2023 the board chair admitted it was "un peu difficile de dire avec précision qui mérite le plus" ([Cameroon Tribune](https://cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/58312/fr.html/details_2)). In September 2026 Henri Lotin, son of Eboa Lotin, posted a **2,000 FCFA** payment for July 2026, and artists called the payouts "aumône" ([allAfrica](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202609230156.html)). SONACAM's own website commits to distribution "en fonction de l'utilisation réelle". It lists "feuilles de détection" among its usage sources and plans BMAT and "Digital Media Service" radio and TV logs ([sonacam.org](https://www.sonacam.org)). Treat the site with care, though. It runs on a third-party builder, gives inconsistent email domains and makes unsourced claims such as "80%" redistributed. It is a statement of intent, not evidence of practice.

## The law makes SONACAM the only possible client and gives usage data a hook

Fair Play cannot sell around SONACAM. A single CMO per category, automatic mandates, and approval that passes through the Prime Minister's office mean that any distribution tool works through SONACAM, MINAC and the CCOGC or not at all. The law also gives Fair Play three footholds, all checked against the statute's text. **Art. 63(1)** requires commercial users of phonograms to give the CMO "les programmes exacts des utilisations" and all the documents needed for distribution. **Art. 78(1)** requires the CMO to keep its repertoire of members and works available to interested persons. **Art. 79(1)(b)** requires it to send its "règles de perception et de répartition" to the minister at least one month before the general assembly examines them ([Law 2000/011](https://oapi.int/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Loi_19decembre_2000Cameroun.pdf)). There is a catch in Art. 63. It sits in the neighbouring-rights title, so for authors' royalties Fair Play's legal basis is SONACAM's own distribution rules (Arts. 78(2), 79). That has a practical consequence. Fair Play's standardisation rule (`sr-1.0`), its sampling method and any base/usage split must be written into SONACAM's distribution rules, adopted by its general assembly and filed with MINAC. A supplier contract alone does not make them binding. The law sets no cap on management costs and no duty to send members itemised statements. The CISAC standard is narrower, but it fits Fair Play: distribution must follow actual usage "or a statistically valid sample" ([CISAC compliance](https://www.cisac.org/compliance)).

Two provisions cut against the team. **Art. 81(1)(e)–(f)** treats irregular performance in an establishment, or late payment of remuneration, as infringement. **Art. 82** punishes infringement with **5–10 years' imprisonment and/or fines of 500,000–10M FCFA** ([Law 2000/011](https://oapi.int/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Loi_19decembre_2000Cameroun.pdf)). Logs from a meter in a bar could therefore become criminal evidence against the bar owner hosting it. **Law No. 2024/017** on personal data was promulgated on 23 December 2024. It creates a data-protection authority, the APDP, and requires prior authorisation for processing. It reportedly entered into force on **23 June 2026** ([Digital Business Africa](https://www.digitalbusiness.africa/cameroun-lautorite-de-protection-des-donnees-a-caractere-personnel-creee/); [Droit Médias Finance](https://droitmediasfinance.com/index.php/actualites/droit-tech-fintech/1297-cameroun-la-loi-sur-la-protection-des-donnees-a-caractere-personnel-entre-en-vigueur-ce-23-juin-2026)). Its implementing decrees may not yet be adopted ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/?c=CM&t=law)). The team did not read the law's text, so Fair Play must not claim compliance. It can claim a design that minimises personal data. Under the law SONACAM would be the controller, Fair Play the processor, and APDP authorisation would come before any field pilot.

## SONACAM keeps the mandate and the money, and Fair Play supplies the split key

The clean boundary follows the CISAC and WIPO Connect model. The CMO owns documentation, shares and statements. A monitoring vendor supplies one more usage source, alongside radio logs ([WIPO Connect](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/wipo-connect); [CISAC formats](https://www.cisac.org/formats)). Ghana's GHAMRO runs exactly this stack: WIPO Connect, ACRCloud monitoring and CIS-Net ([Citi Newsroom](https://www.citinewsroom.com/2026/07/ghamro-to-distribute-over-gh%c2%a21-4m-in-royalties/)). The team found no African CMO using meters inside venues. Fair Play would be a first for the bar category in the region, and it fills the gap SAMRO handles by falling back on "information provided for radio and television" ([SAMRO rules](https://assets-global.website-files.com/641d55c4d3455bd939321315/64387bc3eb8eab3dcca22ee7_SAMRO-Performing-Rights-Royalty-Distribution-Rules.pdf)).

| Function | Today (SONACAM) | With Fair Play |
|---|---|---|
| Membership, identification commission, mandate numbers | SONACAM | Unchanged |
| Work documentation (WIPO Connect, ~105k works claimed) | SONACAM | Unchanged. Fair Play builds a fingerprint reference *from* it, with consent |
| Collection (12 FCFA/crate via distributors, CRTV, licences) | SONACAM and partner CMOs | Unchanged. Meters are never used for enforcement |
| Split key for the bar pot | Board-set flat sums and tiers | **Replaced** by Fair Play shares and ranges, under rules SONACAM adopts |
| Applying rights-holder shares, statements, payment via Express Union | SONACAM | Unchanged. Fair Play's payout screen is an indicative simulation |
| Audit evidence | None published | **Added**: per-play evidence, hash-chained ledger, public hourly root |
| Unidentified music | Invisible | **Added**: an explicit escrow share, never redistributed silently |

What Fair Play should *not* do matters as much. It should not hold or move money. It should not publish authoritative amounts per rights holder, because SONACAM applies its own documentation of shares between authors, composers and publishers. And it should not present its bar-pot result as SONACAM's whole distribution. The demo's "100,000,000 FCFA" pot is safe only because it stays labelled as an example. In reality SONACAM receives an unknown fraction of the levy, after the other four CMOs and possibly the 4% MINAC share. One commercial point deserves a place in the pitch. A panel that logs ISRC (recordings) as well as ISWC (works) serves SCDV's neighbouring-rights pot too. One meter panel can then supply two or more of the five CMOs that share the levy, and that is the realistic route to a viable revenue base.

### The handover is a set of flat files, not a payout

No public layout exists for WIPO Connect's usage import. The safe format is UTF-8 CSV with a header block and usage lines, modelled on DDEX DSR/SURF conventions. Distribution statements in CRD remain SONACAM's output, not Fair Play's ([DDEX DSR](https://kb.ddex.net/implementing-each-standard/digital-sales-reporting-message-suite-(dsr)/); [CISAC formats](https://www.cisac.org/formats)). The design below is the team's inference and should be labelled "prototype format" until SONACAM or WIPO supplies a specification.

| File | Grain | Key fields |
|---|---|---|
| `plays` | One row per confirmed play | play_id, meter_id, venue_id, venue category, city/stratum, started_at (event time, UTC), duration_s, ISRC, ISWC (if known), sonacam_work_code, title, main artist, match confidence, weight, reasons, chain_hash |
| `usage_by_work` | Work × period | period_start/end, ISWC / sonacam_work_code, ISRC(s), raw plays, weighted plays, estimated national plays, share, ci_low, ci_high, flags |
| `run_manifest` | One per stats run | rule version (`sr-1.0`), fp version, strata and bars represented, meters and listening fraction, catch-rate source, bootstrap settings, escrow (unidentified) share, Merkle roots and anchors covered |
| `review_list` | Flagged items | loop and concentration flags, duplicate-suspected uploads, meters that moved (cell_id), late batches |

Most Cameroonian recordings will have no ISRC, and no source confirms that SONACAM uses ISWC. The SONACAM work code must therefore be the primary join key in a pilot, with ISRC and ISWC carried wherever they exist.

## Four risks decide whether SONACAM can say yes

**Politics outweighs technology.** On 6 August 2026 bar owners held a three-day strike called by SYNAPOSOCAM. The union objected that the levy was imposed without consultation ([Glob'Média](https://globmedia.online/2026/08/11/bras-de-fer-entre-les-barmans-et-la-sonacam-12-francs-cfa-de-la-discorde/)). SONACAM defended the levy as ending inspections, seizures and forced closures. A meter on the bar wall looks like the inspector coming back. The board chair threatened in July 2026 to seal bars that do not pay ([Cameroon Tribune](https://www.cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/79271/en.html/copyrights-windfall-sonacam-secures-landmark-100-million-fcfa)), so the fear is credible. Fair Play needs a written firewall: panel data serves distribution only and is never used for licensing, enforcement or Art. 81 complaints. Bars should join voluntarily, through SYNDEBARCAM, which already signed the convention. There is a second political front. Data-driven shares expose how discretionary past distributions were, and that may unsettle the people who chose the payees. The pitch should frame Fair Play as delivering SONACAM's own published promise, not as an indictment of it.

**Data protection must be designed in and stated precisely.** The device sends spectral peaks, one loudness value per window, and a cell ID, never audio. Benchmarks such as Audoo, with PRS and PPL, claim GDPR compliance by not capturing "private conversations", but no independent audits were found ([PPL](https://www.ppluk.com/about-us/news/prs-for-music-ppl-partner-with-music-tech-company-audoo-to-enhance-public-performance-royalty-distributions/)). Fair Play should state what it measured, for example that the 8 kHz peak stream cannot be turned back into speech, only after the team has actually tested it. Venue IDs tied to sole traders and cell IDs that locate them are probably personal data under Law 2024/017.

**Coverage determines whether the shares are true.** The fingerprint index can only match what has been uploaded. SONACAM's repertoire largely sits on deposited CDs, so the reference catalogue has to be digitised with rights holders' consent, starting with the most-played repertoire. The UK warning is concrete. In a Music Venue Trust study, Audoo identified only **21%** of audio captured in grassroots venues ([UK Parliament evidence](https://committees.parliament.uk/writtenevidence/129782/html/)). Fair Play's escrow share for unidentified music is the right answer and should be shown prominently, not hidden. Live bands, DJ pitch shifts and maquis without meters are all coverage gaps, and they belong in the run manifest.

**Sampling must be defensible to the CCOGC.** CISAC and PRS accept "a statistically valid sample of venues" ([PRS](https://prsformusic.com/royalties/music-played-in-public-royalties)). The defence is a stratified panel (city × size), published weights, bootstrap ranges and measured catch rates. The default catch rate of 0.8 must be replaced by calibration before any number leaves the demo. One improvement, the team's inference, ties the panel to the money. The pot is raised per crate, so strata should eventually be weighted by drinks volume rather than by the number of bars, using distributors' outlet sales data if SABC will share it. Regional practice also offers a political bridge: Ghana splits 60% equally and 40% by logged plays ([MyJoyOnline](https://www.myjoyonline.com/ghamro-enhances-royalty-collection-system/)). A configurable base-plus-usage option lets SONACAM move away from flat advances without a sudden shock to its members.

## Conclusion

The research changes the problem from "who collects the bar money" to "who can justify splitting it". The 12 FCFA levy fixed collection and made attribution worse, because volume money arrives without a single playlist. That makes a split key a necessity for SONACAM rather than a nice-to-have, provided it arrives as evidence SONACAM can adopt into its own rules and defend before the CCOGC and MINAC. Fair Play wins as an audited, sampled usage source that SONACAM and SCDV both buy. It loses if it looks like a payment engine, an enforcement device or an outside party deciding artists' pay.

The recommendation, in order of priority, starts with what can be done before Wednesday's 10:00 freeze. **First**, relabel the payout and money screens "indicative simulation: SONACAM applies shares and pays". Keep the pot as an editable example, and make the escrow share visible on the dashboard. **Second**, ship the export as the four prototype files above, with `sonacam_work_code`, ISWC and ISRC columns and a run manifest, and say aloud that it is designed for WIPO Connect import. **Third**, correct the brief's "about 1,480" to show it as derived arithmetic, and add a sources slide marking every external figure as press-reported. **Fourth**, if time allows, add a base-plus-usage split parameter, following the Ghana precedent; otherwise mention it only as a next step. In the pitch, open with SONACAM's own words ("en fonction de l'utilisation réelle", "le meilleur est à venir"). Position Fair Play as SONACAM's paid supplier, and pre-empt the three questions judges will ask: "the meter is not an inspector", "no audio leaves the device", and "a stratified sample with error ranges, not surveillance of every bar". End with a concrete ask: a one-city pilot co-signed by SONACAM, the CCOGC and SYNDEBARCAM, with APDP authorisation. After the hackathon, the blocking questions are the WIPO Connect import specification, the levy's split between the CMOs, the text of the joint decision, access to distributors' outlet volumes, and a consented digitisation of SONACAM's most-played repertoire.
