# Monitoring models, distribution methods and data standards: what "Fair Play" should align with (as of Nov 2026)

Scope note: research done 24 Nov 2026 by web search and fetch. Several primary pages (Music In Africa, Digital Music News, Adomonline, PRS policy PDFs) failed to load, so some findings rest on search-result summaries; these are flagged "(search summary; not fetched)". Vendor and press claims are not independently verified.

## 1. African CMOs: who monitors what, with which technology, and with what results

### Takeaway
Across Africa, monitoring is almost entirely **broadcast** monitoring (radio/TV), sometimes plus digital platforms. **No African CMO found uses in-venue meters in bars or clubs.** Public-performance money (bars, maquis, hotels, transport) is distributed by proxy (broadcast logs), flat or equal shares, or not distributed on usage at all. Ghana (GHAMRO: WIPO Connect + ACRCloud + CIS-Net) is the closest working model. SONACAM (Cameroon) signed for WIPO Connect in 2021 and names BMAT and "Digital Media Service" radio/TV logs as planned inputs. Its recent distributions were flat payments, not usage-based.

### Inferences
- Fair Play would be a **first** in the region: in-venue identification for the bar/maquis category. It fills the exact gap SAMRO rule 6(e) and GHAMRO's "parties are not logged" describe.
- The realistic integration target is a **CMO running WIPO Connect** (SONACAM, GHAMRO, SODAV). Fair Play's output should look like one more usage source next to the BMAT and DMS radio logs, rather than a replacement distribution engine.
- A hybrid split is politically normal in the region: Ghana uses 60% equal and 40% usage-based, and MCSK uses a general layer plus a usage layer. A "base share + usage-weighted share" option in Fair Play's pot calculator matches existing practice and eases the move away from flat payments like SONACAM's 27,000 FCFA.
- Kenya shows the failure modes: opaque vendors, ERP tender scandals, broadcasters not reporting. Transparency features (per-play evidence and per-artist statements, which GHAMRO now advertises) are a selling point.

- Monitoring vendors for SODAV, BURIDA, BBDA, MCSN, SAMRO and SAMPRA. No primary sources were found.
- Whether SONACAM's BMAT and Digital Media Service logs are live, and in what file format. Nothing was found on CRTV logs.
- The identity of "Digital Media Service".

## 2. Public-performance (bar/background music) distribution methods worldwide

### Takeaway
The global norm for background music is **proxy data, usually broadcast or chart data, calibrated by small samples**. A move toward direct data is under way in 2023–2026 through venue meters (Audoo in the UK, DJ Monitor boxes at SACEM) and B2B streaming logs (Soundtrack, about 7,000 UK premises). Known weaknesses: the long tail and local or grassroots repertoire are under-represented, and venue meters have low match rates for unregistered music.

### Cited Findings
- **PRS (UK):** PRS collects performance details from a "statistically valid sample of UK venues" and matches them against similar broadcast data (search summary) — [PRS music played in public](https://prsformusic.com/royalties/music-played-in-public-royalties)
- **PRS broadcast:** for many sampled stations "the sample is augmented by using **Soundmouse** sound recognition to create a virtual census". Soundmouse is used for broadcast, not venues (search summary) — [PRS broadcast policy](https://prsformusic.com/-/media/files/prs-for-music/membership/membership-policies/prs-distribution-policy/july-2023/3prspolicybroadcastrevenue)
- **Audoo (PRS + PPL, announced 9 Aug 2023):** plug-in audio meters in cafés, bars, salons, restaurants and retail. They identify background music and report in "quasi real-time" and are claimed to be GDPR-compliant, not capturing "private conversations". PPL says they complement existing data — [PPL](https://www.ppluk.com/about-us/news/prs-for-music-ppl-partner-with-music-tech-company-audoo-to-enhance-public-performance-royalty-distributions/); [MBW](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/the-uks-prs-for-music-and-ppl-team-up-with-music-startup-audoo-for-public-performance-royalty-allocation/)
- PRS told a parliamentary inquiry it had **167 Audoo devices** in pubs, hairdressers and similar venues. In a Music Venue Trust study, Audoo identified only **21%** of audio captured in grassroots venues, which is not the same as 21% being unregistered (search summary) — [UK Parliament written evidence](https://committees.parliament.uk/writtenevidence/129782/html/)
- 2026: Music Venue Trust and Audoo partnered to test how grassroots-venue music is reflected in distributions. The venue count is reported as 120 (another report says 12) — [Digital Music News, 9 Jun 2026](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2026/06/09/mvt-audoo-partnership-prs-proxies/); [Record of the Day](https://www.recordoftheday.com/on-the-move/news-press/grassroots-music-venues-take-centre-stage-in-new-music-venue-trust-and-audoo-partnership)
- **PRS + Soundtrack:** exact-play data from about 7,000 UK premises, to be used in royalties from **Dec 2026** (search summary) — [MBW](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/prs-for-music-partners-with-b2b-streaming-service-soundtrack-to-get-data-on-music-played-in-7000-uk-shops-bars-and-public-spaces-and-pay-royalties-accordingly/)
- **SACEM (France):**
  - Since 2018 SACEM has funded boxes from **DJ Monitor** (Netherlands) in clubs and festivals. The boxes encode tracks for recognition "sans enregistrer les lives". The reported recognition rate is above 80% for tracks registered at SACEM and in the DJ Monitor database (2017–2020 sources; search summary).
  - Discothèques without a box are covered by **sample surveys on a fixed panel of clubs** set up by a polling institute.
  - The search found no BMAT or Yacast role at SACEM.
  - [Tsugi](https://www.tsugi.fr/un-track-joue-en-club-combien-ca-rapporte-a-son-auteur/); [Raje](https://raje.fr/article/comment-la-sacem-fait-elle-pour-reconnaitre-les-morceaux-joues-en-club-ou-festival); [Electronic Music Factory](https://electronicmusicfactory.com/le-monitoring-un-enjeu-toujours-essentiel-pour-la-retribution-des-artistes-travers-la-france)
- SACEM's distribution rules have dedicated sections for "Discothèques et bars à ambiance musicale" and "Sonorisation" (April 2025 edition) — [SACEM règles de répartition 2025](https://www.sacem.lu/media/33bee4f4-de46-4202-97d0-4d64f8201150/regles-de-repartition-avril-2025-fr.pdf)
- Cost benchmark: a 700-capacity French discothèque pays about €20,000 a year to SACEM — [Assemblée nationale QE](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/15/questions/QANR5L15QE31291.pdf)
- **SAMRO:** a broadcast proxy for "General" when there is no venue data (see section 1) — [SAMRO rules](https://assets-global.website-files.com/641d55c4d3455bd939321315/64387bc3eb8eab3dcca22ee7_SAMRO-Performing-Rights-Royalty-Distribution-Rules.pdf)
- **CISAC standard:** the compliance checklist (Rules 18A–18C) requires distribution based on actual usage **or a statistically valid sample** (search summary) — [CISAC compliance](https://www.cisac.org/compliance); [Professional Rules](https://www.cisac.org/services/business-and-governance/professional-rules)
- **Brazil, as a comparator:** Crowley monitors radio for ECAD — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crowley_Broadcast_Analysis)

### Inferences
- Fair Play's statistical framing should be "a **statistically valid sample of venues**". That wording meets the CISAC rule and mirrors PRS and SACEM, so not every bar needs a meter. Design for a stratified panel (city, venue type, size) plus extrapolation weights, and publish the sampling method.
- Expect low match rates for local Cameroonian repertoire unless the reference database is seeded from SONACAM's documentation and local releases. Report "unidentified share" explicitly (the Audoo 21% lesson) instead of silently dropping it.
- Data from Fair Play could also act as a correction factor on a broadcast proxy, which is how PPL frames Audoo ("complement").

### Gaps
- GEMA, SOCAN, ASCAP and BMI venue methods were not researched for lack of time.
- The current PRS public-performance policy PDF could not be retrieved (404).

## 3. Data standards and what SONACAM would most likely ingest

### Takeaway
The identifiers that matter are **ISWC (works), ISRC (recordings), IPI (parties)**, exchanged through **CIS-Net**. Formats: **CWR** for work registration, **CRD** for distribution statements, and **DDEX DSR/SURF**-style flat files for usage. WIPO Connect (SONACAM's system) imports "usage reports or data capture", but no public spec of its import format was found. The safest bet is a delimited flat file (CSV/TSV) keyed on ISRC/ISWC with title, artist, venue, timestamp and duration, modelled on DDEX DSR flat-file conventions.

### Cited Findings
- **WIPO Connect Local** covers registering rights holders, documenting works and recordings, licensing, "identifying and matching used works, with usage reports or data capture", and producing distribution reports. **WIPO Connect Shared** handles cloud sync, submitting to industry databases, assigning industry identifiers and sharing repertoire with foreign CMOs. The page names no specific file format and lists no deployments — [WIPO Connect](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/wipo-connect)
- Under a 2020 WIPO–CISAC agreement, WIPO Connect CMOs share repertoire with other CMOs via CIS-Net — [WIPO news 2020](https://www.wipo.int/copyright/en/news/2020/news_0001.html)
- WIPO is integrating ISNI into WIPO Connect — [IFRRO Board Report 2025](https://ifrro.org/resources/documents/General/IFRRO_Annual_Board_Report_2024_25.pdf)
- CISAC's CIS toolset: identifiers ISWC, ISAN, IPI and ISNI; tools CIS-Net, the IPI System and AV Index; formats **CWR, AVR, UP, CRD, CAF**. **CRD** is the format for reporting royalty distributions between societies and from a society to its members — [CISAC formats](https://www.cisac.org/formats); [CISAC information services](https://www.cisac.org/services/information-services)
- A field-level CRD example is PRS's CRD 4 spec (2025), which includes ISWC and period-of-account fields — [PRS CRD4](https://www.prsformusic.com/-/media/files/prs-for-music/membership/crd-2025-updates/crd4-rd-4-0-format-v2)
- CIS-Net 2.0 renewal is targeted for 2027 (secondary source) — [Downtown Music Africa](https://downtownmusic.africa/where-collections-stand-and-how-africa-measures-up/)
- **CWR** is the CISAC works-registration exchange format, with a lighter "CWR Light" variant. CRF020 is a lookup-table file, not a usage format — [CWR docs](https://cwr-dataapi.readthedocs.io/en/latest/cwr_standard/documents.html)
- **DDEX DSR** is a flat-file (delimiter-separated) sales and usage report from service to rights owner, first published in 2016. **SURF** is a simpler, Excel-friendly DDEX format accepted by many societies, including The MLC — [DDEX DSR](https://kb.ddex.net/implementing-each-standard/digital-sales-reporting-message-suite-(dsr)/); [The MLC specs](https://themlc.com/specifications-templates-and-guidelines); [MLC DSRF guide](https://www.themlc.com/hubfs/Marketing/Website%20Files/Guidelines%20for%20sending%20monthly%20Reports%20of%20Usage%20to%20The%20MLC%20using%20DDEX%20DSRF%20v1.4%20v1.1.2.pdf)
- No CISAC-defined standard format for broadcast or venue monitoring reports was found.
- GHAMRO now cites WIPO Connect, CIS-Net and the CISAC Professional Rules as its stack, and that shows the target alignment for an African CMO — [Citi Newsroom 2026](https://www.citinewsroom.com/2026/07/ghamro-to-distribute-over-gh%c2%a21-4m-in-royalties/)

### Inferences
- **Recommended Fair Play export** (inference, not a confirmed SONACAM spec): one row per detected play, as UTF-8 CSV/TSV, following the DSR/SURF idea of a header block plus usage lines. Fields:
  - venue_id
  - venue category (bar / maquis / club / hotel)
  - city / GPS cell
  - start_utc and duration_s
  - ISRC, ISWC (if known), title, main artist
  - match_confidence
  - SONACAM work code / WIPO Connect work ID (if mapped)
  - meter_id
  - period_start / period_end
- Add an aggregated per-work file (plays and seconds per period). WIPO Connect then only needs matching, and SONACAM keeps control of the shares and the CRD-style statements.
- Fair Play should **not** compute final per-rightsholder money as the authoritative output. Under the CISAC model the CMO applies documentation (shares, IPI) inside WIPO Connect, so Fair Play should give an "indicative share by artist/work" for the demo and an "official usage report" for the CMO.
- Fair Play needs both identifiers: ISRC keys the fingerprinted recording, and ISWC keys the composition that SONACAM (authors' rights) pays. A Cameroonian recording without an ISRC needs a fallback ID, such as SONACAM's own work code.

### Gaps
- The WIPO Connect usage-import file layout. It is not public, so ask WIPO or SONACAM directly.
- The format of BMAT's usage reports to CMOs. No public spec was found (BMAT reports are believed to be CSV/Excel-style but this was **not verified**).

## 4. Privacy and cost of venue meters

### Takeaway
Industry meters claim privacy by **on-device fingerprinting with no raw audio leaving the device and no non-music capture** (Audoo is "GDPR-compliant"; SACEM's DJ Monitor boxes don't record the live performance). Independent audits were not found. Public hardware prices were not found. Cloud recognition (ACRCloud) is priced per monitored stream per month (about US$26/month reported second-hand) or custom-quoted, and an on-premises "Local Monitoring Tool" exists.

### Cited Findings
- Audoo describes its meters as smart plug-in, GDPR-compliant devices that report music in quasi-real-time "without capturing any non-music audio, whether that's the weather report or private conversations". ### Inferences
- Fair Play privacy design should match the industry claim and make it verifiable:
  - fingerprint on the device and discard the audio buffer within seconds
  - send only hashes and match IDs
  - optionally gate on a music-vs-speech classifier
  - publish this design
  
  This is directly defensible against the Audoo/GDPR benchmark. Cameroon's data-protection framework was not researched here.
- Bar connectivity is intermittent, so Fair Play needs **store-and-forward**: queue fingerprints or matches locally and sync over GSM/Wi-Fi. A local or offline matcher against a seeded Cameroonian catalogue would also cut per-stream cloud costs (see the ACRCloud Local Monitoring Tool).
- Per CMO practice (SACEM), the CMO or sponsor funding meters in a sample panel is a precedent. Bars do not have to buy them.
