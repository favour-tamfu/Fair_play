# Cameroon's legal framework for collective management of music copyright (SONACAM): collection and distribution, as of Nov 2026

Primary text used: the French text of Law No. 2000/011 of 19 December 2000, as hosted by OAPI (PDF dated April 2025). I extracted the text locally and read the articles directly: https://oapi.int/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Loi_19decembre_2000Cameroun.pdf (WIPO Lex record: https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/fr/legislation/details/836). For the implementing decree (Decree No. 2001/956/PM), I used the WIPO Lex French PDF, read through a summarizing fetch tool, so the decree is not quoted verbatim: https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/edocs/lexdocs/laws/fr/cm/cm008fr.pdf (record: https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/fr/legislation/details/7757).

## Law No. 2000/011: public performance, CMOs, distribution, transparency, private copying, sanctions

### Takeaway
Law 2000/011 is still the base statute in 2026. I found no replacement law and no amendment to it. It sets these rules:
- One CMO per category (Art. 75).
- Joining a CMO automatically gives it a mandate to license, collect, distribute and sue (Art. 77).
- A CMO must keep its repertoire of members and works available to interested persons (Art. 78(1)).
- A CMO must use its revenue according to a scale (barème) set in its statutes and approved by the Minister of Culture (Art. 78(2)).
- A CMO must give MINAC its accounts, its collection and distribution rules, and its auditor's reports (Art. 79).
- Commercial users of phonograms must give the CMO "les programmes exacts des utilisations" and all the documentation needed for distribution (Art. 63).

The law contains no explicit rule requiring distribution "in proportion to use" and no numeric cap on management costs. Those details are left to statutes and ministerial texts.

### Cited Findings
**Exclusive rights and public performance**
- Art. 15: the author's economic rights give an exclusive right to exploit or authorise exploitation "sous quelque forme que ce soit". They cover representation, reproduction, transformation, distribution and the droit de suite. Art. 15(3) gives royalty claims the same legal status as wage claims ("même régime que les créances salariales") — [Law 2000/011, OAPI](https://oapi.int/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Loi_19decembre_2000Cameroun.pdf)
- Art. 16(1): "représentation" means communicating a work to the public, including making it available on demand. It covers (a) public recitation, dramatic performance and public execution "par tous moyens ou procédés" and (c) broadcasting by wire or wireless means. Playing recorded music in a bar falls under this — [Law 2000/011](https://oapi.int/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Loi_19decembre_2000Cameroun.pdf)
- Art. 41(3): organising shows requires an authorisation, and the organiser must pay a fee set by regulation — [Law 2000/011](https://oapi.int/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Loi_19decembre_2000Cameroun.pdf)
- Art. 7(4): unless proven otherwise, the author is the person under whose name or pseudonym the work is declared to the competent CMO. This makes CMO registration evidence of authorship — [Law 2000/011](https://oapi.int/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Loi_19decembre_2000Cameroun.pdf)

**Neighbouring rights: equitable remuneration for public use of phonograms, and the users' data duty**
- Art. 60: once a phonogram is released commercially, performers and producers cannot object to its direct communication in a public place (unless it is used in a show) or to its broadcasting — [Law 2000/011](https://oapi.int/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Loi_19decembre_2000Cameroun.pdf)
- Art. 61(1)-(2): that use gives performers and producers a right to remuneration.
  - It is paid by the people who use phonograms for commercial purposes.
  - It is based on operating revenue ("assise sur les recettes d'exploitation") or, failing that, assessed as a flat fee ("évaluée forfaitairement").
  - It is split 50/50 between performers and producers.
  — [Law 2000/011](https://oapi.int/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Loi_19decembre_2000Cameroun.pdf)
- Art. 62: the competent CMO sets the tariff and payment terms in consultation with users. If no agreement is reached within six months, or when an earlier agreement expires, an arbitration commission rules with final effect — [Law 2000/011](https://oapi.int/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Loi_19decembre_2000Cameroun.pdf)
- **Art. 63(1)** is the key data provision. Commercial users of phonograms must, when they pay, give the competent CMO "les programmes exacts des utilisations auxquelles elles procèdent et tous les éléments documentaires indispensables à la répartition des droits". Art. 63(2) says the CMO collects the remuneration on behalf of rights holders and distributes it among them — [Law 2000/011](https://oapi.int/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Loi_19decembre_2000Cameroun.pdf)
  - The duty to report exact usage sits in the neighbouring-rights title (Title III). I found no equivalent explicit article for authors' public-performance royalties, the part SONACAM collects as an authors' society.

**Private copying (Title IV)**
- Art. 69-71: authors, performers and producers of works fixed on phonograms or videograms are entitled to a private-copying remuneration.
  - It is paid by the manufacturer or importer of blank recording media when the media are put into circulation in Cameroon.
  - The amount depends on the type of medium and its recording time.
  - Rates are set by regulation.
  - The competent CMO collects it.
  - Art. 71(3) splits it in **equal shares among authors, performers, producers and the cultural-policy support fund** (one quarter each).
  — [Law 2000/011](https://oapi.int/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Loi_19decembre_2000Cameroun.pdf)
- Art. 72-74: private copying of printed works is paid by manufacturers or importers of reprographic machines and split in equal thirds among authors, publishers and the support fund — [Law 2000/011](https://oapi.int/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Loi_19decembre_2000Cameroun.pdf)

**Collective management (Title V)**
- Art. 75(1)-(3): rights holders may create CMOs. "Il ne peut être créé qu'un organisme par catégorie de droit d'auteur et de droits voisins", with categories defined by genre. Rights holders keep the option to exercise their rights directly — [Law 2000/011](https://oapi.int/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Loi_19decembre_2000Cameroun.pdf)
- Art. 76: the rules for controlling how CMOs are created and run are set by regulation — [Law 2000/011](https://oapi.int/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Loi_19decembre_2000Cameroun.pdf)
- Art. 77:
  - Authors, performers, phonogram and videogram producers, publishers and their successors may be members.
  - Unless agreed otherwise, joining a CMO gives it a mandate for all acts of collective management, including licensing, "la perception et la répartition des redevances", and legal defence.
  — [Law 2000/011](https://oapi.int/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Loi_19decembre_2000Cameroun.pdf)
- Art. 78(1): CMOs must "tenir à la disposition des personnes intéressées le répertoire de leurs membres et des oeuvres de ceux-ci". Art. 78(2): they must use their revenue according to a barème set in their statutes and other core texts approved by the Minister of Culture — [Law 2000/011](https://oapi.int/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Loi_19decembre_2000Cameroun.pdf)
- Art. 79(1): each CMO must send the Minister of Culture, on its own initiative or on request:
  - (a) its annual accounts;
  - (b) changes to its statutes and to its "règles de perception et de répartition des droits", at least one month before the general assembly examines them;
  - (c) cooperation agreements with third parties;
  - (d) general-assembly decisions;
  - (e) balance sheets, reports and the auditor's report (commissaire aux comptes);
  - (f) the names of its representatives.
  Art. 79(2): the Minister may also collect this information on documents and on site — [Law 2000/011](https://oapi.int/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Loi_19decembre_2000Cameroun.pdf)
- Art. 96: existing CMOs had 12 months to comply. Art. 97 repeals Law No. 90/010 of 10 August 1990 — [Law 2000/011](https://oapi.int/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Loi_19decembre_2000Cameroun.pdf)

**Cultural-policy support fund**
- Art. 5(4) creates a "compte de soutien à la politique culturelle". It receives:
  - folklore fees (Art. 5(3));
  - public-domain fees (Art. 39(2));
  - one quarter of private-copying remuneration on phonograms and videograms (Art. 71(3)) and one third on printed works (Art. 74(3)).
  — [Law 2000/011](https://oapi.int/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Loi_19decembre_2000Cameroun.pdf)

**Sanctions (Title VI)**
- Art. 80 defines infringement (contrefaçon), including any exploitation by representation in breach of the law and any unauthorised communication to the public of phonograms — [Law 2000/011](https://oapi.int/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Loi_19decembre_2000Cameroun.pdf)
- Art. 81(1) treats the following as infringement. Two points matter directly for venues:
  - **(e)** "le fait de laisser reproduire ou de représenter dans son établissement de façon irrégulière les productions protégées";
  - **(f)** "le défaut de versement ou le retard injustifié de versement d'une rémunération prévue par la présente loi".
  It also covers circumventing technical protection measures (d) and removing rights-management information (g) — [Law 2000/011](https://oapi.int/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Loi_19decembre_2000Cameroun.pdf)
- Art. 82: penalties are **5 to 10 years' imprisonment and/or a fine of 500,000 to 10,000,000 FCFA**. They are doubled if the offender is the rights holder's contracting partner — [Law 2000/011](https://oapi.int/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Loi_19decembre_2000Cameroun.pdf)
- Art. 84-90 provide:
  - confiscation of infringing copies, equipment and the receipts they produced;
  - publication of the judgment;
  - seizure by police officers or bailiffs;
  - court orders to suspend unlawful public performances (Art. 85(2)(b)) and to seize receipts (85(2)(e));
  - customs suspension of goods (Art. 90).
  — [Law 2000/011](https://oapi.int/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Loi_19decembre_2000Cameroun.pdf)

### Inferences
- Art. 81(1)(e)-(f) and Art. 82 make an unlicensed bar, or one that does not pay, criminally liable. This is SONACAM's main enforcement lever.
- Art. 63 is the clearest statutory basis for requiring venue and broadcaster usage logs (playlists, "programmes exacts"). A usage-monitoring tool could present itself as a way for users to meet this existing duty. Strictly, Art. 63 is worded for phonogram remuneration (neighbouring rights). For authors, the basis would be SONACAM's statutes and distribution rules (Art. 78(2), 79(1)(b)).
- The law asks for rules to be filed and approved, not for distribution "in proportion to use". Fairness of distribution therefore depends on the approved distribution rules and MINAC decisions, not on the statute itself.

### Gaps
- The official English version (WIPO cm001en) could not be downloaded. Only the French text was verified.
- I found no explicit statutory cap on management costs or social-fund percentage, and no explicit duty to send individual statements to members.

## Decree No. 2001/956/PM and later regulatory texts (2015 amendment, 2017 SONACAM approval, 2020 MINAC decisions, control commission)

### Takeaway
Decree 2001/956/PM sets out the CMO categories:
- A: literature and drama;
- B: music;
- C: audiovisual and photographic arts;
- D: graphic and plastic arts.

It also sets the conditions for approval (agrément) and the arbitration commission. It contains no rules on management-cost caps, distribution keys or audits. The decree was amended on 22 May 2015 so that the Minister's approval decisions now need the Prime Minister's visa. SONACAM was approved for category B in December 2017. In June 2020, MINAC signed 10 of 12 new decisions on collection and distribution. A Commission de Contrôle des Organismes de Gestion Collective (CCOGC) oversees CMOs, but I could not find its founding text.

### Cited Findings
- Decree 2001/956/PM of 1 Nov 2001 was signed by PM Peter Mafany Musonge. It implements Law 2000/011 — [WIPO Lex record](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/fr/legislation/details/7757); [French PDF](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/edocs/lexdocs/laws/fr/cm/cm008fr.pdf)
- Decree provisions, as summarised from the WIPO PDF:
  - Art. 2: folklore fees are set by order of the Minister of Culture.
  - Art. 3: the droit de suite is 5% of the resale price.
  - Art. 8: public-domain fees are 50% of the usual royalty.
  - Art. 9: organising shows requires authorisation from the Minister of Culture.
  - **Art. 10: organisers of paid-entry shows pay 10,000 FCFA to the cultural-policy support fund.**
  — [Decree PDF](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/edocs/lexdocs/laws/fr/cm/cm008fr.pdf)
- Arts. 11-12: the Arbitration Commission (for Law Art. 62).
  - It is chaired by a judge appointed by the President of the Supreme Court.
  - Members: a MINAC representative, one per rights-holder organisation, and one per category of phonogram user.
  - Two-year terms, renewable; a two-thirds quorum; the chair has a casting vote.
  — [Decree PDF](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/edocs/lexdocs/laws/fr/cm/cm008fr.pdf)
- Arts. 13-17: private-copying rates for phonograms and videograms are set by order of the Minister of Culture (the rates are not in the decree). Reprographic machines carry 5% of the sale price — [Decree PDF](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/edocs/lexdocs/laws/fr/cm/cm008fr.pdf)
- **Art. 18**: one CMO per category, with categories A, B, C and D as listed above (B = musical art) — [Decree PDF](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/edocs/lexdocs/laws/fr/cm/cm008fr.pdf)
- **Arts. 19-21**: CMOs need approval from the Minister of Culture. Conditions:
  - a non-profit civil company or legal person;
  - a seat in Cameroon;
  - managers and founders of proven competence and integrity;
  - payment of **500,000 FCFA** to the cultural-policy fund;
  - membership open to all rights holders in the category.
  The application file includes statutes, a list of managers and criminal records. The Minister decides within 60 days — [Decree PDF](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/edocs/lexdocs/laws/fr/cm/cm008fr.pdf)
- **Art. 22 (original 2001 wording)**:
  - approval lasts **5 years**, is renewable and cannot be transferred;
  - a breach leads to formal notice to comply within one month;
  - approval can be suspended for up to 30 days;
  - it can be withdrawn only for repeated breaches;
  - decisions must give reasons.
  — [Decree PDF](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/edocs/lexdocs/laws/fr/cm/cm008fr.pdf)
- **22 May 2015 amendment**: the PM signed a decree amending Art. 22(7) of Decree 2001/956/PM. Approval is now "refusé, accordé, suspendu ou retiré par acte motivé du ministre chargé de la Culture, après visa du Premier ministre". The PM also annulled MINAC's approval of SOCACIM dated 18 May 2015. The amending decree's number was not given — [Camer.be, 25 May 2015](https://www.camer.be/42355/11:1/cameroun-le-pm-donne-le-tempo-sur-le-droit-dauteur-de-lart-musical-cameroon.html)
- WIPO Lex lists only the 2000 law and the 2001 decree for Cameroon copyright. The 2015 amendment and the MINAC decisions are **not** on WIPO Lex — [WIPO Lex Cameroon profile](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/fr/legislation/members/profile/CM)
- **SONACAM approval**: SONACAM was approved in December 2017, reportedly "valable pour une période de dix ans renouvelable". This conflicts with the 5-year term in the original Art. 22. Either the term was changed by a later text I could not find, or the press figure is wrong — [Cameroun24](https://cameroun24.net/article/43439-cameroun-les-artistes-disent-merci-pour-l-agrement-de-la-sonacam.html); [Cameroon Tribune](https://www.cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/20174/fr.html/details_2)
- SONACAM has held the category B (music) approval since 2017. The rival Cameroon Music Corporation (CMC) lost its approval. The Supreme Court ruled for the government in CMC's challenge, and CMC lost its provisional CISAC membership in 2023. A 2014 presidential directive to clean up the music CMO sector is mentioned — [Cameroon Tribune, 20 Jul 2023](https://cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/58771/fr.html/details_2)
- **Control commission**: the "Commission de contrôle des organismes de gestion collective (CCOGC)", which works under MINAC's supervision, was chaired by **Théophile Eyango** in July 2023 — [Cameroon Tribune, 20 Jul 2023](https://cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/58771/fr.html/details_2). In April 2026 the CCOGC president was reported as **Pr. François Edimo** — [Cameroon Tribune, 28 Apr 2026](https://cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/76231/fr.html/details_2). I did not find the founding text, a decree or an arrêté. The brief's name "CPMC" (Commission Permanente de Médiation et de Contrôle) appears to be an earlier or alternative name. I could not confirm this.
- **June 2020 MINAC package**: after a review that began in January 2020, Minister Bidoung Mkpatt signed **10 of 12 texts** on the collection and distribution of copyright and neighbouring-rights royalties on 12 June 2020. They include a decision approving a scale for sharing royalties between socio-professional groups for certain users, and a decision on how CRTV shares the royalties it owes. A new neighbouring-rights society (Société civile camerounaise des droits voisins, SCDV) raised the number of CMOs from four to five — [Cameroon Tribune, 18 Jun 2020](https://cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/33111/fr.html/details_2)
- The five CMOs active today are SONACAM (music), SCAAP, SOCILADRA, SOCADAP and SCDV — [Cameroon Tribune, 20 Jul 2023](https://cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/58771/fr.html/details_2)
- MINAC reviewed CMO performance in April 2026. Four distributions took place between April 2025 and April 2026 — [Cameroon Tribune, 28 Apr 2026](https://cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/76231/fr.html/details_2)
- The only broadcaster that "really" pays is CRTV, according to Cameroon Tribune. Targets for wider collection include private broadcasters, mobile operators (ringback tones, downloads, streaming), web media and GECAM member companies — [Cameroon Tribune, 8 May 2026](https://www.cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/76554/en.html/collecte-du-droit-dauteur-une-assiette-dusagers-elargir). CRTV settling its debt is reported at [AllAfrica, Apr 2025](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202504080188.html).
- I found no draft law to replace Law 2000/011. An expert interviewed by Cameroon Tribune says the law needs adjusting for digital issues and that the structure of collective management should be reconsidered — [Cameroon Tribune](https://www.cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/76558/en.html/-notre-loi-necessite-adaptations-pour-mieux-saisir-les-enjeux-du-numerique-). A separate August 2026 interministerial draft concerns cinema, not copyright — [Cameroon Tribune](https://cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/79754/fr.html/details_2)

### Inferences
- The binding rules on distribution keys, management-cost deductions and social or cultural funds probably sit in MINAC decisions (notably the June 2020 package) and in SONACAM's approved statutes and general rules. None of these is published online in full text.
- The 2015 amendment centralised approval power with the Prime Minister. Combined with the one-CMO-per-category rule, this means SONACAM is a state-sanctioned monopoly for music. Any data tool would have to work through SONACAM, MINAC and the CCOGC.

### Gaps
- I did not find: the number of the 2015 amending decree, the CCOGC's founding text and powers, the full texts of the June 2020 decisions, any percentage cap on management costs or social/cultural deductions, or SONACAM's distribution rules. These are possibly in the Journal officiel or held by MINAC, and the press articles are paywalled.
- The approval term is unresolved: 5 years (decree 2001, Art. 22) versus "10 years" (2017 press).
- The decree was read through a summarising fetch tool, not verbatim. The article numbering should be checked against the PDF.
- I found nothing on 2023-2025 regulatory reforms.

## Legal basis linking beverage sales to copyright, and fees for bars and drinking establishments

### Takeaway
There is no statutory beverage tax or excise share earmarked for copyright. The link is administrative and contractual:
- a MINAC decision of 15 April 2009 set flat fees for bars by type and size;
- breweries pay those fees by withholding them from the rebates they pay bars;
- a joint MINAC-MINCOMMERCE decision, implemented through a contract with SABC (Boissons du Cameroun), now provides for withholding at source.
The "compte de soutien à la politique culturelle" is funded by statute through folklore, public-domain, private-copying, show and approval fees. It does not receive beverage money.

### Cited Findings
- On 15 April 2009, the Minister of Culture signed a decision setting the rate and collection method for the copyright and neighbouring-rights fee owed by bars and drinking establishments.
  - Fees range from **29,500 to 85,000 FCFA**, depending on the type of establishment (take-away, bar, snack bar, bar-dancing) and its size.
  - An earlier decision had set **15 FCFA per crate**, which proved hard to apply.
  - Under an agreement announced on 18 June 2009, the **breweries pay the fee and recover it by deducting it from the rebates ("ristournes") owed to bars**.
  - SOCAM (SONACAM's predecessor) acted as agent for the three other CMOs (SOCILADRA, SOCADAP, SCAAP), each taking a share under a distribution grid.
  — [237online, 19 Jun 2009](https://www.237online.com/droit-dauteur-la-collecte-des-fonds-facilite-auprs-des-tenanciers-des-bars/)
- A contract with Société anonyme des Boissons du Cameroun (SABC) implemented a **joint ministerial decision (MINAC and MINCOMMERCE)** that withholds at source the fees owed by bars and drinking establishments. The date and number of that decision were not visible — [Cameroon Tribune, 28 Apr 2026](https://cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/76231/fr.html/details_2)
- SONACAM reportedly recovered about 100 million FCFA through the at-source withholding with Boissons du Cameroun — [Cameroon Tribune (EN)](https://www.cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/79271/en.html/details_2) (title only seen; not read in full)
- In 2020 the bar owners' union (Syndebarcam) contested which body collects the fee and how it is calculated — [Cameroon Tribune](https://cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/35250/fr.html/details_2) (search snippet only)
- Artists dispute their payouts, for example the "Eboa Lotin: 2,000 FCFA in royalties" controversy of September 2026 — [AllAfrica, Sep 2026](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202609230156.html) (search snippet only)
- The cultural-policy support fund receives money only from the sources named in the law (Art. 5(4), 39(2), 71(3), 74(3)) — [Law 2000/011](https://oapi.int/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Loi_19decembre_2000Cameroun.pdf) — and from the decree's show fee (Art. 10) and CMO approval fee (Art. 20) — [Decree PDF](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/edocs/lexdocs/laws/fr/cm/cm008fr.pdf)

### Inferences
- Bar fees are a flat fee tied to the type and size of the establishment, collected through the beverage supply chain. Art. 61(2) allows a flat fee, but the fee has no connection to what music is actually played. Distribution of this pool therefore cannot reflect actual bar playlists unless separate usage data (for example sampling) is collected. This is the core argument for a "Fair Play"-type data tool.
- The breweries are collection intermediaries only. Their legal duty comes from the MINAC/MINCOMMERCE decision and the contract, not from a finance law.

### Gaps
- I found no finance-law or excise provision earmarking a share of beverage revenue for copyright. I found no source tying the copyright fee to a compte d'affectation spéciale. The 2024 and 2026 finance-law documents (dgb.cm) were not reviewed in detail.
- I did not find the full text, number or date of the joint MINAC-MINCOMMERCE decision, or the current bar tariff (it may have been updated in the June 2020 package).

## Data obligations (repertoire, usage data, published rules, member statements) and data protection (Law 2024/017)

### Takeaway
The statute requires three things on data:
- the CMO's repertoire of members and works must be available to interested persons (Art. 78(1));
- users of phonograms must supply exact usage programmes (Art. 63);
- collection and distribution rules and accounts must go to MINAC (Art. 79).
The statute does **not** require public publication of distribution rules or individual statements to members. Separately, Law No. 2024/017 of 23 December 2024 on personal data protection entered into force on about 23 June 2026, after an 18-month transition. It creates a data-protection authority (APDP) and requires prior authorisation for processing. Recording audio in bars would need to be assessed under this law.

### Cited Findings
- Art. 78(1) (repertoire available), Art. 63(1) (exact usage programmes and documentation needed for distribution), Art. 79(1)(b) (collection and distribution rules sent to the Minister) and Art. 79(1)(a), (e) (annual accounts and auditor's report) — [Law 2000/011](https://oapi.int/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Loi_19decembre_2000Cameroun.pdf)
- Art. 81(1)(g)-(2) protect "information sur le régime des droits" (rights-management information that identifies the work and its terms of use, including codes). Removing it is treated as infringement — [Law 2000/011](https://oapi.int/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Loi_19decembre_2000Cameroun.pdf)
- Law No. 2024/017 was adopted by the National Assembly on 26 November 2024 and promulgated on 23 December 2024.
  - It creates the Autorité de protection des données à caractère personnel (APDP).
  - It gives an 18-month compliance period.
  - It requires prior authorisation for processing.
  - It applies to controllers and processors established in Cameroon.
  — [Digital Business Africa](https://www.digitalbusiness.africa/cameroun-lautorite-de-protection-des-donnees-a-caractere-personnel-creee/); [Digital Business Africa](https://www.digitalbusiness.africa/?p=86769)
- The law reportedly entered into force on 23 June 2026 — [Droit Médias Finance](https://droitmediasfinance.com/index.php/actualites/droit-tech-fintech/1297-cameroun-la-loi-sur-la-protection-des-donnees-a-caractere-personnel-entre-en-vigueur-ce-23-juin-2026)
- The implementing decree and ministerial orders were reported as not yet adopted. That page is undated, so this may be out of date — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/?c=CM&t=law)

### Inferences
- A bar audio-capture device that only fingerprints music, without storing raw audio or voices, would limit personal-data processing. Capturing conversations could still count as processing personal data under Law 2024/017 and may need APDP authorisation. This needs verification against the law's text.
- Art. 63 gives an existing legal hook for collecting usage data from users. Art. 78(1) supports a CMO repertoire database that can be matched against usage.

### Gaps
- I did not read the text of Law 2024/017 itself (articles on sensitive data, audio and video surveillance, sanctions). I found no statutory duty for CMOs to send itemised statements to members, and no rule on publishing distribution rules to the public.

## OAPI / Bangui Agreement and international treaties

### Takeaway
Cameroon is party to:
- the Berne Convention (1960);
- the Bangui Agreement / OAPI (1982);
- TRIPS (1995);
- the Marrakesh Treaty (2022);
- the WCT and WPPT, both in force for Cameroon since 9 April 2025.
It has signed but not ratified the Beijing Treaty. The Rome Convention is not listed.

### Cited Findings
- Treaty dates from WIPO Lex:
  - Berne: in force 1 Jan 1960.
  - Bangui Agreement (OAPI): 8 Feb 1982.
  - TRIPS: 13 Dec 1995.
  - Marrakesh: 5 Jan 2022.
  - **WCT: 9 Apr 2025.**
  - **WPPT: 9 Apr 2025.**
  - Beijing: signed 26 Jun 2012, not ratified.
  - Rome: not listed.
  — [WIPO Lex Cameroon profile](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/fr/legislation/members/profile/CM)
- Accession to a WIPO copyright treaty was authorised by Law No. 2024/004 of 24 July 2024 — [Lextenso, L'Essentiel Droits africains, 2024](https://www.labase-lextenso.fr/l-essentiel-droits-africains-des-affaires/2024-n10/cameroun-adhesion-au-traite-de-l-ompi-sur-le-droit-d-auteur-DAA202q2)
- Under Law 2000/011, Art. 93-94, foreigners are protected on a reciprocity basis and works protected under treaties to which Cameroon is party are covered. This is the basis for distributing to foreign repertoire through reciprocal agreements, which require CISAC links. SONACAM was invited to apply for CISAC membership in 2023 — [Law 2000/011](https://oapi.int/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Loi_19decembre_2000Cameroun.pdf); [Cameroon Tribune 2023](https://cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/58771/fr.html/details_2)

### Inferences
- Joining the WPPT strengthens the international basis for performers' and producers' remuneration for public use of phonograms (Law Art. 61). Law 2000/011 already anticipated on-demand making available (Art. 16, 59).

### Gaps
- I did not verify the text of Annex VII of the Bangui Agreement (literary and artistic property), its 1999 revision or the 2015 Bamako revision, or whether those apply in Cameroon. Annex VII generally sets minimum standards and leaves collective management to national law, but this is unverified and was not sourced in this research.
- I did not confirm whether Cameroon's WCT/WPPT accession requires changes to Law 2000/011 (for example on technical protection measures, which Art. 81(1)(d) already covers).
